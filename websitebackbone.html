Reading the image. Dark areas are smooth surfaces, such as open water, that bounce the radar signal away from the satellite. Bright areas are rougher ground, vegetation and buildings. Look for where dark areas grow or shrink between two dates.

About the data
  • Product: NISAR L2 GCOV (calibrated radar backscatter), L-band, track 019, descending pass.
  • Scenes: 27 Jun, 9 Jul, 21 Jul, 14 Aug, 7 Sep, 1 Oct and 6 Nov 2026. This track has no scene between 21 Jul and 14 Aug.
  • The flood peak date (19 Jul) comes from news reports, not from the radar data.
  • Display: backscatter in decibels as greyscale (HH from −25 to 0 dB, HV from −32 to −5 dB).
  • Processing: scenes were averaged to a 40 m grid so that different native resolutions match (10 m on 9 Jul, 21 Jul and 14 Aug, 20 m on the others), then cropped to the area all seven scenes cover. Edge strips with no useful signal were trimmed.
  • Limits: darkness is a visual guide, not a measured flood area. Wind can brighten open water, and smooth dry surfaces such as sand can look dark.

Contains modified NISAR data (NASA/ISRO), produced by NASA JPL and distributed by the ASF DAAC. Basemaps: Esri and OpenStreetMap contributors.
