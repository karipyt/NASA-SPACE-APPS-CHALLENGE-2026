    Project overview

    This map visualizes the 2026 monsoon on a large braided river: how surface water spreads, how channels shift, and how the floodplain goes under and dries out again.

    The images are L-band Synthetic Aperture Radar from the NASA-ISRO NISAR mission, using its GCOV backscatter product. Radar sees through monsoon cloud, and smooth open water returns very little signal, so water reads as dark against brighter land.

    Why the Jamuna, and why not Kaziranga

    The first target for this application was Kaziranga National Park and the Brahmaputra floodplain in Assam, India.

    Querying the 2026 NISAR GCOV archive across pre-monsoon and monsoon passes showed a problem: the satellite's orbital footprints over Kaziranga did not overlap enough from one date to the next across the target river reach. Without the same ground covered in every scene, a reliable before-and-after comparison could not be built.

    Moving downstream to the Jamuna River in Bangladesh (Track 019, descending) gave a consistent, overlapping stack of seven scenes from June to November 2026. That stack captures the early-monsoon braided channels, the mid-July flood peak, and the post-monsoon recession of the water.
